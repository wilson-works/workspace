# Working well with AI at a firm

AI is a fast, tireless assistant that has read a great deal and knows nothing about your clients,
your firm or your day until you tell it. These habits get good work out of it and keep the firm safe.
Where the firm has its own rule on something, the firm's rule wins: follow it.

## Ask well

- **Give it the context a new colleague would need.** Who it is for, what it is for, what "good"
  looks like, and any limits. "Draft a reminder email" gets a generic email; "Draft a short, friendly
  reminder to a small-business client that their bank statements for October are due Friday, from me,
  no jargon" gets one you can send.
- **Say what you want back.** A checklist, a table, three options, one paragraph, a draft email.
- **Point it at the file.** "Use the PDF in `00-Inbox`" beats pasting pieces of it into the chat.
- **Ask it to ask you.** "Ask me any questions you need before you start" saves a wrong first draft.

## Trust, then check

- **Ask for sources.** For any accounting, tax or regulatory question, ask Louise, or ask "give me a
  source for every fact". A fact with no source is a guess until you check it.
- **Check every number** against the original document. AI is good at reading and arranging numbers,
  and still makes mistakes copying, adding and rounding them.
- **Watch dates and recent changes.** Rules, limits and deadlines change. Check the date on a source.
- **"Not verified" is a good answer.** It means the agent could not find a source and told you so
  instead of guessing. Thank it, and find the fact yourself.
- **You sign off, not the AI.** Anything that goes to a client, a tax authority or the books is your
  work. Read it as if you wrote it.

## Keep client data safe

- **Keep client files where the firm keeps them.** Follow the firm's rules on where client documents
  may be stored and which client information may be used with AI.
- **Never paste client information into other AI tools or websites.** Use the firm's own setup: your
  Workspace, Tony, Balnce.Pro and the CBFC Academy.
- **Use codes, not names,** in task titles, boards and notes where you can.
- **Bring in only what the task needs,** through `Hub/00-Inbox`, and tidy it away when you are done,
  as the firm's rules say.
- **The Hub fence is a guard, not a vault.** It keeps Claude inside your Hub. It does not make it a
  good place to keep client files for longer than the task needs.

## Work in small pieces

- **One task per chat.** Start a new chat for a new task. Old details from a long chat can mix into
  new answers.
- **Break big jobs into steps.** "First list what's missing from this checklist. Then we'll draft the
  email." Check each step before the next.
- **Ask for a plan before changes.** "Show me what you'll change before you change it." Every prompt
  in the firm library does this.
- **Put the work on your board.** A task on a board with a block on your calendar gets done. A task in
  a chat's memory does not.

## Save what works

- **Keep your best prompts.** When a prompt gives you exactly what you wanted, save it: in your own
  notes in the Hub, or ask "save this prompt to my prompt notes". The
  [firm prompt library](../../prompts/firm.md) is a starting point.
- **Turn a repeated job into an agent.** If you paste the same long prompt every week, that job wants
  its own agent, with its rules and facts written down once. The course's last lesson shows how.

## Teach it

- **One mistake, one fix.** When an agent gets something wrong, tell it exactly what and ask it to
  make one fix so it doesn't happen again: a rule, a step, or a missing fact. Small fixes add up.
- **Give facts their source.** "Our month-end checklist is the one in the firm library" is better
  than a fact with nowhere to check it.
- **Write fences in plain words.** "Never email a client without my yes." Agents follow plain rules
  well.

## When to ask a person

- **Judgment calls** about a client, a filing position or anything with a penalty attached.
- **When two sources disagree,** or the only source is the AI itself.
- **Anything that moves money** or sends something outside the firm. Tony never moves live money, and
  you shouldn't let any agent do it either.
- **When something feels off.** Stop the chat, take a screenshot, and ask the office manager.
