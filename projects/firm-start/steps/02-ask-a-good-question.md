The difference between a vague answer and a useful one is almost always the question. In this lesson you ask the same thing twice, once loosely and once well, and see the difference. Then you check the answer the way a reviewer would.

## What you'll get

- A side-by-side of a loose ask and a good one.
- The habit of asking for the format you want, and for questions back.
- One prompt saved for later.

## Do this

**1. Ask loosely.** In a chat on your Hub, paste:

```
Write an email asking for documents.
```

**2. Ask well.** In a new chat, paste:

```
Draft a short, friendly email to a small-business client asking for their October bank and credit card statements by Friday. From me, plain words, no jargon, under 120 words. Leave <CLIENT NAME> as a blank. Give me two versions: one warm, one brief. Ask me any questions you need before you start.
```

Compare the two. The second says who it is for, what it is for, how long, what tone, and what to give back.

**3. Ask about a rule.** Paste, choosing a topic you know well enough to judge:

```
Explain <a rule or term you already know, e.g. accrual vs cash basis> in plain words, for a new bookkeeper. Then one worked example with made-up numbers, and name your source.
```

Read it as a reviewer. Is it right? Is the example's math right? Does it name a source you could check?

**4. Save the prompt that worked.** Paste:

```
Save the email prompt I used in my second chat to a file called my-prompts.md in 50-AI, with a one-line note on when to use it. Show me the file.
```

## What you should see

- The loose ask gives a generic email. The good one gives two drafts you could nearly send, or asks you a question first.
- An explanation with an example and a named source. If it has no source, ask "Where does that come from?"
- `50-AI/my-prompts.md` with your first saved prompt.

## If something's off

- **The answer was confidently wrong.** That happens. It is why you check, and why the next lesson is about sources.
- **It wrote a long essay.** Say how long you want it: "under 120 words", "five bullets".

## Done when

- [ ] You have compared a loose ask with a good one.
- [ ] You checked an explanation, its math and its source.
- [ ] `50-AI/my-prompts.md` holds your first saved prompt.
